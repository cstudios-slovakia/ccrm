# CCRM Version 1.9-Jackfruit Changelog

## [1.9.69-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-18

### 🐛 Fix: status dropdowns overflowing the automation trigger card

The "From Status" / "To Status" selects on the Lead/Task Status Changed
trigger, and the Priority select on the Create Task action, sat in a narrow
two-column grid inside a fixed-width automation node card. A long status
label kept the select's flex item from shrinking, so its truncation never
kicked in — the label ran past the card edge and clipped the dropdown's
chevron instead of ellipsizing.

- Added `min-w-0 flex-1` to those `CustomSelect` triggers so they shrink
  inside their flex row and the label truncates properly.

## [1.9.68-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-18

### 🐛 Fix: a recurring rule's paid amount now drives its forecast

Editing the **paid amount** ("Skutočná suma") of a recurring rule changed what
its own ledger row and the Pravidelné výdavky tab showed, but not what the
Prehľadová tabuľka, the trend and the "Budúce pohyby" rows expected for the
coming months — those priced every charge at the *planned* amount whenever one
was set. Rent budgeted at 2 000 € and actually paid at 2 200 € stayed at
2 000 € in every forecast, and the edit form still promised "the new amount
applies from the next charge".

- A charge is now worth the paid amount when there is one, and the planned
  amount only until something has been paid — the same rule the ledger row and
  the recurring tab already used, so all five finance tabs agree.
- The amount history still pins the old price: raising the paid amount today
  re-prices next month's charge and leaves the months already paid alone.
- The edit form's "new amount applies from the next charge" hint only appears
  when the figure the forecasts use actually moved, and the "earlier amounts"
  list is priced the same way.

## [1.9.67-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-18

### 🎨 Finance: the income / expense form reads top to bottom

The create/edit form for a financial movement mixed field heights, label styles
and backgrounds, and its sections came in no particular order — assignment sat
between the title and the category, the amounts came before the dates they
belong to, and several labels wrapped onto two lines.

- New order: **type → title & document number → category → status & dates →
  amounts → recurring switch → assignment → note**.
- Every field shares one label style and one 40 px input style (white, hover
  border, emerald focus ring); the category picker gained a matching `md` size.
- Shorter labels: "Kategória" (was "Klasifikácia kategórie (3 úrovne)"),
  "Číslo dokladu", "Dátum vystavenia", "Poznámka"; amounts show the € inside
  the field.
- The recurring row is one big switch — click anywhere on it — with a one-line
  explanation, and turns indigo while on.
- Assignment (Celá firma / Projekt / Klient) is a segmented control like the
  income/expense switch, with the project or client picker under it.

## [1.9.66-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-18

### 🐛 Fix: a card's own "Newest / Highest value" toggle reloaded the whole Dashboard

Flipping a table widget's own view toggle (Najnovšie leady's "Newest / Highest
value", the tasks table's "Mine / Whole team") re-ran every widget's query, not
just that card's: the effect that decides what to (re)fetch keyed off whether
the *entire* widget array's queries had changed at all, so one card's query
changing was enough to refetch and flash the loading overlay over every widget
on the board at once.

- The refetch now diffs each widget's (and each tab's) query against what was
  last fetched and only re-fetches the ones that actually changed.
- A widget that already has data on screen no longer gets blanked out by the
  loading overlay on a refetch — the old rows stay up until the new ones are
  ready.
- The leads and tasks table widgets animate row changes instead of snapping:
  rows that persist between the two views glide into their new order, rows
  that leave fade out and the ones that arrive fade in.

### ✨ A stretched leads/tasks table card fills with more rows

The "Rows" setting on the leads and tasks table widgets was a fixed count —
stretching the card taller (a bigger widget size, more room in the grid row)
left the extra space blank under the last row instead of showing more data.

- It is now a **minimum**: the table still shows at least that many, but as
  many more as fit in however tall the card actually ends up.
- The widget fetches up to 30 rows regardless of the setting, so there is data
  on hand to fill a stretched card with.

## [1.9.64-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### ✨ The movements ledger can show what has not happened yet

Finance → Pohyby only ever showed what had been entered. A recurring rule is
kept as a single record, and an invoice issued in August sits in August however
far away its due date is, so nothing on the page answered the question the page
is for: what is next month going to cost, and what is coming in?

- **"Budúce pohyby" draws the forecast into the ledger**, in the same table and
  the same chronology as the real movements, so an expected charge sits among
  the ones that already happened instead of in a report of its own. Every source
  of a future transaction is walked: each charge a recurring rule still has
  ahead of it, priced at the amount in force on that day; every unpaid movement
  with a due date ahead of us, the invoice payable next month included; and
  anything entered for a day that has not arrived yet.
- **An expected row never reads as a settled one.** Violet, on a dashed rail,
  with an approximate amount, the day it is expected and how far off that is,
  and where it was derived from in place of the payment status it cannot have.
  It has no status picker and no delete button, because there is nothing stored
  to change or remove — only a way back to the rule or invoice behind it.
- **Nothing is counted twice.** An invoice the forecast moves forward to its due
  date stops being drawn on the day it was issued, and the month subtotals and
  the summary pills keep expected money in a pill of its own, apart from the
  figures that mean money that really moved.
- **One month at a time.** A recurring rule charges forever, so the forecast is
  bounded: it opens one month ahead, says how far it reaches, and offers the
  next month only while there is something in it to load. Once every rule has
  run out and every invoice is accounted for, it says so instead of offering an
  empty month.
- The forecast obeys the filter bar exactly as the ledger does — search, type,
  category, scope, value range and date preset all apply to an expected row, and
  another month is only offered when the filters would actually let it through.
- The maths lives in `utils/futureMovements.ts` and is covered by unit tests;
  `tests/e2e/financialFutureMovements.spec.ts` pins the overlay in the running
  app.

## [1.9.63-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### 🐛 The overview table counts every movement, categorised or not

"Skutočnosť" in the finance overview table was lower than in the movements
ledger and the cash-flow trend, and stayed that way until every single payment
had been filed under a category: the table simply skipped any movement without
one, and skipped it from "Total Expenses", "Total Incomes" and the net row too.

- **An "Uncategorized" row in each section.** Movements with no category, with
  a category that has since been deleted, or with a category from the other
  side of the ledger, now have a row of their own under the expense and income
  categories. It is part of the section totals, and it filters like any other
  row.
- **A partial payment shows what was really paid as real.** A 9 200 € invoice
  with 4 000 € received used to show 4 000 € as an *estimate* and nothing real,
  in the table and in the trend; it now shows 4 000 € real and the remaining
  5 200 € as still expected.
- **A paused recurring rule charges nothing.** The table and the cash-flow
  projection kept charging a rule marked paused every month, while the
  recurring tab's totals left it out. The three now agree.
- **Deleting a category keeps its movements.** They stay in every tab as
  uncategorized instead of vanishing from the table.
- **Switching a movement between income and expense drops a category from the
  other side**, so an income can no longer be saved under an expense category.
- The table's arithmetic — which row a movement lands on, what counts as real,
  how the three category levels roll up — moved to `utils/financialOverviewTable.ts`
  and is covered by unit tests; `tests/e2e/financialOverviewTable.spec.ts` pins the
  row in the running app.

## [1.9.62-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### 🐛 Changing a recurring expense no longer rewrites what it cost last month

A recurring movement is kept as one rule, not as a row per charge, so every
report rebuilt its past charges from the rule's *current* amount. Raising the
rent from 500 € to 600 € therefore re-priced every month that had already been
paid at 500 €: the weeks behind the current one in the cash-flow projection, and
the past columns of the overview table, all silently moved to the new figure.

- **A new amount applies from the next charge onwards.** Editing the amount of a
  recurring rule — or entering what was really paid straight from the movements
  ledger — pins the previous amount to everything already charged. The form says
  so before you save, and the rule's row shows which date its current price has
  been in force since, with the earlier ones in the tooltip.
- **Successive changes each keep their own stretch of history.** A rule that went
  500 € → 600 € → 750 € shows each figure in the months it was actually charged
  in, and the cash-flow curve behind the current week stops moving every time
  somebody corrects a price.

### 🐛 Recurring charges are counted off the calendar

The same reports estimated *how often* a rule charges instead of working it out,
which quietly bent the totals:

- A monthly rule set to the 31st was skipped in every month shorter than that —
  it now charges on the last day of those months.
- A "2nd Tuesday of the month" rule was read as if the week number were a day of
  the month, so it landed on the wrong date; it now falls on the right weekday,
  as does "last Friday".
- A yearly rule was counted once in *every* quarter and half-year column of the
  overview table, instead of only in the one containing its month. A weekly rule
  was counted as a flat four times a month, never five.
- The day of the month chosen for a yearly rule was ignored and treated as the
  1st.
- "Next charge" in the recurring tab is read off the same calendar as the
  projections, so the two always agree, and a rule that has passed its end date
  says it has no further charges instead of inventing one.

## [1.9.61-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### ✨ Drop a dashboard card into the empty space beside another one

Rearranging the Dashboard only ever worked by dropping a card *on* another
card. Empty cells refused the drop outright — the cursor turned to "not
allowed" over them — so the unused half of a row could only be filled by
reordering cards until one happened to land there.

- **Free cells are now drop targets.** While a card is in the air, every run of
  empty cells it fits in is outlined, and the one under the pointer fills in.
  Let go and the card takes exactly those cells; nothing else on the board
  moves.
- **Width decides what is offered.** A quarter-width card can be parked in the
  six unused columns at the top of a board; a two-thirds-width one cannot, and
  is simply never offered that gap instead of being dropped somewhere
  unexpected.
- **The card follows the cursor.** Dragging lifts the card out of the board and
  carries it under the pointer, shadowed and tilted, while the cards left
  behind close the space up. `Esc` puts it back.
- **The board animates instead of jumping.** Reordering a card, resizing one,
  or dropping one into a gap now slides every affected card from where it was
  to where it belongs — including the card that changed width, which grows
  into its new size rather than snapping to it.

## [1.9.59-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### ✨ Each project type lays out the projects table itself

The projects list showed the same eight columns to everyone, in an order nobody
chose, and the custom attributes a project type is built around — the roof area,
the budget, the variant — could not be seen from the list at all. You had to
open a project to read the one number you were scanning for.

- **A Columns tab in each project type's settings.** Built-in columns and that
  type's own attributes sit in a single list: a switch turns each on or off, and
  a drag (or the up/down buttons) puts them in the order you want.
- **Custom attributes can be columns.** Each is rendered in the shape its type
  deserves, but compact enough for a table row: money formatted in its own
  currency, dates localised, a multi-option checkbox as chips, a file slot as a
  count, a contact as the person's name.
- **Attribute columns sort like any other.** Numbers compare as numbers, money by
  its amount, a contact by the name rather than the id stored behind it. A
  project with a blank value sorts last in both directions, as an empty deadline
  already did. They are offered in the sort dropdown too, so the card view can
  be ordered by one as well.
- **The project name stays first and cannot be switched off** — it is what
  identifies the row and opens it.
- The layout applies once the list is showing that one type: filter the list by
  it, or have no other type defined. A list of **mixed types falls back to the
  built-in columns**, because an attribute column means nothing to a project of
  another type.
- Changing a type does not disturb an arrangement: deleting an attribute removes
  its column, a **newly added attribute is offered but stays off**, so adding a
  field never silently widens everyone's table. A project type nobody has
  arranged shows exactly the table it always did.
- Stored per type in `project_types.list_columns_json`. A client older than this
  release sends no layout at all, and the server keeps what is stored rather
  than flattening it — the same rule the type's file slots already follow.

## [1.9.58-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### ✨ The Dashboard was redesigned from a grid of cards into one panel

The Dashboard used to render every widget the same way: a white box, a title,
and whatever the generic metric / chart / table renderer made of the rows.
That is the right fallback for a card an AI invented, and the wrong one for the
nine cards the app ships with, whose queries are fixed and whose meaning is
known. Those nine are now drawn by renderers written for them.

- **The lead table** shows each lead as the app shows it everywhere else: an
  initials disc, the name, whether it is a company or a person and the town
  underneath, the phase as a badge in the colour Settings gave that phase, the
  value right-aligned, the manager as a pill. Its heading carries a
  **Newest / Highest value** switch, and its footer says how many of how many
  leads are on screen and what they add up to. Rows open the lead.
- **The task table** shows the task with the lead it belongs to underneath, the
  assignee, the state badge, and a deadline chip that **turns red once it is
  overdue**. Its heading carries a **Mine / Whole team** switch.
- **Leads by stage** is a real ring with the total in the middle and a legend
  carrying the counts, drawn from the configured phase colours rather than from
  a chart palette. **Leads by source** and **Tasks by status** likewise.
- **Open tasks** now says how many are blocked and shows how the open ones
  split across the states.
- Every card wears the **icon and accent of the section its data comes from** —
  the sales board's blue, the task board's orange — so the panel reads as a
  summary of the app instead of a colour chart.

### ✨ Editing a dashboard is direct, and each widget has its own settings

- **Edit mode** outlines every card, faintly draws the twelve columns behind
  them, and puts a small toolbar over each: drag to reorder, the width it is
  at, settings, duplicate, remove. The corner grip cycles the width.
- **Widget settings** opened from a card (or by clicking it) is a drawer: its
  name, the section it reads from, its width as four pictures of the grid, how
  it is ordered, how many rows, which phases or states it reports, and — for
  the two tables — **which columns it shows and in what order**, by dragging
  them.
- **Add widget** lists the library grouped by app section with a search box,
  says what kind of card each one is, and marks the ones already on the board
  as added.
- The **L width is now two thirds** of the grid rather than a half, which is
  what lets a table sit beside a stacked pair of quarter cards. The starter
  layout uses it.

### 🔧 Behind it

- `recent_leads` and `recent_tasks` return what the cards draw — the town and
  client type, the related lead and project, the category — and take the
  widget's own ordering, row count, phase and assignee filters as parameters.
  Both carry the unfiltered total so a card can say "5 of 44".
- The card's own switches filter without putting the dashboard into an unsaved
  state; only a change made in the settings drawer does that.

## [1.9.57-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### 🐛 The CRM marked every incoming email as read, even ones nobody had opened

A user with a connected mailbox found messages showing as read in their mail
client that they had never opened, there or in the CRM. The cause was the way
the CRM fetched message bodies: a plain IMAP body fetch makes the mail server
set the `\Seen` flag as a side effect, and the CRM fetched bodies in three
places that have nothing to do with reading:

- the **background inbox poll** every two minutes, which caches each new
  message's text for the AI assistant — so mail was marked read within minutes
  of arriving, whether or not the Email module was even open;
- the **AI summary pre-fetch** for every message in the list;
- the client and lead **timeline lookups**, and attachment downloads.

Every one of those fetches now *peeks* (`FT_PEEK`), which leaves the flag
alone. The only thing that marks a message read is a person opening it.

### ✨ Read state now follows the mail client both ways

- **Read in the client, read in the CRM.** The list already refreshed the flag
  from the server every minute; it still does, and a message whose body the
  CRM had cached is now re-marked read if the client had flipped it back to
  unread and you open it again.
- **Read in the CRM, read in the client.** Opening a message sets the flag by
  its stable UID (the way the mail client addresses it) and the CRM shows the
  flag the server reports back, not the one it assumed.
- **Mark as read / unread by hand.** The message header and the thread header
  gained a toggle. On a thread, "read" clears every unread message and
  "unread" lifts the newest one, which is what mail clients do for a
  conversation. The server confirms each change before the list reflects it.
- The AI summariser no longer falls back to treating a UID as a sequence
  number when the message is gone, which could summarise a different message
  after a deletion.

## [1.9.56-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-17

### ✨ Projects carry a star rating, like leads do

A lead has always had a 1-5 star priority you could set from the list and
filter by. A project had nothing — every project looked equally urgent, and
"which of these actually matters this week" was a question the list could not
answer.

- **Rate a project from the list.** A Rating column in the table and a row of
  stars on each card, clickable straight from either — no edit mode, no Save.
  Clicking the star a project already wears clears the rating again.
- **The same rating on the project card**, next to its status, saved the moment
  you click it.
- **Filter by it.** The projects filter bar gained the leads list's rating
  dropdown, with exactly the same options: all, four-and-up, three-and-up, each
  exact rating, and "not rated".
- **Sort by it.** "Rating" joins the sort menu and the table's sortable column
  headers. A project nobody has rated sorts last whichever direction you pick —
  it has no priority, rather than the lowest one.
- Unlike a lead, which is born at three stars, a project starts **unrated**: the
  list never claims a priority nobody set. Every project that existed before
  this release is therefore unrated, not one-star.
- Under the hood the star row and the filter's rules now live in one place each
  (`ui/StarRating`, `utils/rating`), shared by the leads list and the projects
  list, so "★★★★ and up" cannot come to mean two different things.

### 🐛 A preference set during the QA audit reverted mid-test

The mocked backend threw every write away, including the signed-in user's
`metadata_json` — which is where every interface preference lives. The
background poll then replaced the local profile with the server's pristine
copy, so a sort order or view toggle set by a test silently undid itself a few
seconds later and the test read as an app defect. The fixture now hands back
the user metadata it was given.

## [1.9.51-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-16

### 🐛 Four more edits that silently reverted or never saved

A round-trip audit of `sync.php` (`docs/audits/sync-roundtrip-audit-2026-09-16.md`)
found four more spots where an edit looked like it worked but was quietly
dropped on the next sync.

- **Timeline entry date/time reverted after ~1 minute.** Correcting only the
  time on a phone call, appointment or status-change entry (its text
  unchanged) was discarded by the server's identity check, which never
  compared `timestamp` (or `author`). It now does, with the same
  date-normalisation the write path already uses.
- **VAT verification result was never cached.** A standalone save of a
  client's VAT check result touched only `vat_validation_result`, which the
  identity check also didn't compare — so the write was always skipped and
  the app re-ran the external VIES lookup every time the client was opened.
  The identity check now compares the decoded JSON.
- **Documents attached from the Clients screen 404'd after a reload.**
  `ClientsView` set a timeline event's `filePath` but never mirrored it into
  `attachments` (the field that actually survives a sync round trip), and the
  screen's own fallback URL didn't match how the file was actually stored on
  disk. Fixed to mirror `attachments` the same way `LeadsDatagrid` already
  does, across all three renderers that build the download link.
- **A repeat web-form inquiry could vanish if the lead was edited seconds
  later.** Filing a second inquiry against an existing lead only touched
  `leads.updated_at` when a previously-empty field got filled in — normally a
  no-op for a lead with full contact details — so the sync conflict guard had
  no signal that the timeline changed, and a concurrent edit's timeline
  replace could delete the new inquiry note. The parent lead's `updated_at`
  is now always bumped when the note is inserted.
- Also silently fixed: an unrelated settings save (e.g. changing the system
  name) was overwriting `CUSTOM_LABELS` with `{}` every time, because nothing
  in the UI sends that field yet. It now follows the same "omitted means
  unchanged" contract as its neighbours.

## [1.9.50-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-16

### 🐛 Lead creation date could not be changed

Editing the creation date of an existing lead (or client) looked like it
worked, then the old date came back on the next sync.

- **Root cause:** the server's lead upsert in `sync.php` set `created_at`
  only when inserting a new row; the `ON DUPLICATE KEY UPDATE` branch never
  touched it, so the database kept the original date and the next pull
  overwrote the local edit.
- **Fix:** the update branch now writes `created_at` as well. A payload that
  carries no `createdAt` keeps the stored date instead of being stamped with
  today's date. Verified against the real MySQL schema on a throwaway lead.

## [1.9.49-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-16

### 🧪 QA suite repairs

### 🚀 `php ccrm update` follows the branch the install is actually on

An install that was not on `main` could not update itself. `ccrm update` pulled
`main` unconditionally, so a checkout sitting on a feature branch was told to
merge an unrelated branch into itself and died on git's `Need to specify how to
reconcile divergent branches` — and the hint git prints there invites the
operator to "fix" it by setting `pull.rebase`, which would genuinely have
rebased or merged `main` into a live site.

The deploy branch is now resolved from the most explicit source that exists:

1. `CCRM_DEPLOY_BRANCH=<branch>` — a one-off override for a single invocation.
2. `git config ccrm.deployBranch <branch>` — persistent, and the right way to
   pin a box permanently. It lives with the checkout, so it also holds for cron

3. **the branch that is actually checked out** — a checkout on X updates from

4. `main`, only when HEAD is detached and git can say nothing.

before it happens rather than after. Production sits on `main` and behaves
exactly as it did.

The pull is now `--ff-only`. A deployment checkout has no history of its own, so
anything that is not a fast-forward is a real divergence, and the update stops
and says which branch it is on versus which it was told to pull — instead of
either a wall of git hints or a quiet merge commit on a customer's site.

## [1.9.16-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-07

### ⚡ Performance — the company dropdown is instant again

Typing a company name got noticeably slower after the lookup started using the
RPO register (api.statistics.sk), and the measurements say it was not a local
impression. Same machine, same minute, five runs per query:

| Query | RegisterUZ | RPO |
|---|---|---|
| name "eset" | median 0.14 s | median 3.88 s |
| name "elektro" | median 0.14 s | median 13.49 s |
| by IČO | 0.15 s | 0.25 s |

The delay is the register thinking, not the network or the payload: the TLS
handshake is ~0.15 s to both, and everything else sits in time-to-first-byte.
"tatra banka" returns 4 KB and still takes 8.9 s. RPO also ignores `limit` — a
search for "eset" returns every match in full, with all name variants since
1990, 251 KB of it — and roughly one call in four stalls for exactly 5.03 s,
a constant flat enough to be a throttle rather than load.

Because both registers were queried in one request, a name search cost the
slower of the two, and the worst case was worse than that: RPO regularly
overran the 9-second cap and was **discarded**, so a search for "elektro" made
you wait nine seconds and then showed no sole traders at all — the very rows
RPO had been added for.

The two registers are now asked separately and each reply is rendered as it
lands. The list appears in about 0.1 s, exactly as it did before RPO existed,
and the sole traders drop into it a few seconds later, correctly ordered,
because every suggestion now carries the `rank` the backend scored it with —
the client merges by sorting on that number instead of re-implementing the
scoring rules. A slow or failing register can no longer empty a list the other
one already filled, and RPO alone gets a 25-second budget, so its results
arrive late rather than not at all.

Measured through the endpoint the browser calls, cold cache:

| You type | Before | After (first rows) |
|---|---|---|
| `eset` | 9.06 s | **0.09 s** |
| `elektro` | 9.07 s, no sole traders | **0.11 s** |
| `tatra` | 5.61 s | **0.10 s** |
| `31333532` | 0.30 s | 0.30 s |

Identifier lookups were never the problem and are untouched. Czech lookups go
to a single register and still travel in one request.

### 🔧 Fixed — the printed offer really is light now, and its ink budget is sane

The previous release made the printable price offer a fixed light document, but
on a running dev server nothing changed: printing from a dark-themed app still
produced a dark sheet. The mechanism was right and the delivery was broken.

enough, because PostCSS re-imports the config with a cache-busting query but
Node's module cache still hands back the plugin it loaded at boot. Verified
live: renaming the class the plugin emits changes the stylesheet the browser is
served, without killing `npm run dev`.

## [1.9.14-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-07

### ✨ Added — one company lookup everywhere, sole traders included

Typing a company name into the *Register client* drawer has filled the form from
the register for a while. Two things were wrong with it: it only ever asked
RegisterUZ (registeruz.sk), which knows the entities that file accounts — so a
**živnostník** simply never appeared, no matter how the name was spelled — and
it was the only form in the app that could do it at all. The company's own
billing identity, a warehouse supplier and the client block on an invoice were
all typed out by hand.

`/api/company_registry.php` now queries **RPO** (api.statistics.sk) alongside
RegisterUZ, in parallel, and merges the two on IČO. RPO carries both the
Obchodný register (orsr.sk) and the Živnostenský register (zrsr.sk), so
companies and sole traders resolve the same way, and it is the only source with
a readable legal form ("Spoločnosť s ručením obmedzeným", not the code `112`),
the statutory body and the current address. RegisterUZ contributes the DIČ and
the statistical codes RPO does not publish. Czech lookups still go to ARES,
through the same normalised response.

The lookup now runs in four places, from four fields each — **name, IČO, DIČ and
IČ DPH**:

- **Clients** — the register drawer and the client profile panel. Picking a
  match fills the address, legal form, SK NACE, region, district, organisation
  size, establishment date and, where the form has not got one yet, the
  statutory body as the contact person.
- **Settings → Invoicing** — the company's own identity block, which had no
  lookup at all. The address underneath fills itself, and the country it needs
  to pick a register is now an editable field rather than an invisible one.
- **Warehouse → Suppliers** — the *Auto-Fill* button still works with a bare
  IČO, and the four fields now suggest as you type. Its own copy of the
  RegisterUZ/ARES plumbing is gone; it calls the shared lookup.
- **Invoicing → the client step** — a lead captured without its IČO or full
  address can be completed from the register while the document is being
  written. The match is written back to the client in the CRM, not just onto the
  document.

Three things the shared code guarantees, in every form:

**A blank from the register never overwrites what is stored.** Sole traders have
no published DIČ; losing a hand-typed one to an empty field would be worse than
not filling it at all.

**The suggestion the user meant is at the top.** An exact IČO or DIČ hit ranks
first, then a name that starts with what was typed, then one that merely
contains it; dissolved entities sink to the bottom. Punctuation and diacritics

**Each row says where it came from** — an `OR SR` or `ŽR SR` badge, plus a
*Dissolved* marker where the entity no longer exists.

Both register calls run in one request through curl_multi and are cached on disk
(6 h for searches, 24 h for details), so the slower of the two sets the wait

## [1.9.13-Jackfruit] — 2026-09-07

### 🔧 Fixed — a printed price offer is a document again, not a screenshot

Printing an offer produced a page with the browser's own date and title across
the top, its URL and page counter across the bottom, the document squeezed into
the middle of the sheet — and, if the app was in dark mode, the whole thing in
dark mode too. Worse, the document was *cropped*: the right-hand metadata column
and the entire footer block never reached the paper, and a long itemised offer
came out as a single cut-off sheet.

Three separate causes, all in the print stylesheet:

**The crop.** Everything except the document was hidden with `visibility:
hidden`, which hides the app shell but leaves it occupying space. The document
therefore had to be positioned absolutely over the top of it — and its
containing block was the preview modal, so it was sized and clipped by
`max-h-[90vh]` / `overflow-hidden` instead of by the sheet. Now everything that
is neither the document, nor an ancestor of it, nor inside it is taken out of
the layout entirely, the surviving wrappers are stripped of their positioning
and scrolling, and the document sits in normal flow from the top of the first
sheet and paginates by itself.

**The headers and footers.** There is no CSS switch for those; Chrome draws them
in the page margin, so a page box without one leaves them nowhere to go. `@page`
is now `margin: 0` and the whitespace around the document is the document's own
`10mm` padding — which is also what lets it run closer to the edges of the sheet
than the previous 14mm page box allowed.

**The theme.** Dark mode here re-points the Tailwind palette variables on
`<html>` (see `scripts/postcss-dark-palette.mjs`), so a document written in
`bg-white text-slate-900` follows the app into the dark like everything else. A
printable document should not: it is white paper. The palette plugin now also
emits a `.force-light` block — every variable the dark theme root sets, put back
to its light value — and the offer/invoice templates carry that class. The
document is white with dark type whatever the app is set to, on paper *and* in
the preview, so what you see before pressing Print is what comes out.

The block is generated rather than hand-written, for the same reason the rest of
the dark-mode overrides are: a variable added to the dark theme next month is
undone inside the document without anyone remembering a list.

## [1.9.5-Jackfruit] — 2026-08-28

Licensing. An installation now needs a valid licence key **to receive updates** —
and that is the only thing a licence controls. Nothing in the running CRM is
disabled by an expired, missing, or even revoked licence: no feature is hidden,
no data withheld, no login refused. A customer whose licence lapsed keeps a fully
working app and simply stops receiving new versions until they renew.

### ✨ Added — the licence itself

- **Settings → Licence** (`LicenseSettings.tsx`), a new category in System
  Preferences. Shows the installed key (masked), who it is licensed to, the plan,
  the expiry date with days remaining, seats used out of seats licensed, when the
  licence server last confirmed it, and whether updates are currently allowed.
  Administrators can enter a key, force a re-check, and remove the licence.
- **One field takes two things.** A licence key (`CCRM-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX`) and a
  signed *offline token* — for an installation with no outbound internet — go in
  the same box; the backend tells them apart by shape. A key pasted with the
  wrong case, stray spaces, or no hyphens is normalised rather than rejected.
- **An advance-warning banner** (`LicenseBanner.tsx`) above the workspace,
  starting 30 days before expiry (the licence can carry its own window). It is
  deliberately not a modal and not a toast — nothing here justifies interrupting
  someone mid-task, and the whole point of warning early is that there is still
  time. Every wording carries the reassurance that the CRM is unaffected.
  - **Close** hides it for the browser session.
  - **Don't show again** hides it until the *situation* changes — a renewed key,
    or "expires soon" becoming "expired", brings it back. The preference is
    stored per user in `users.metadata_json.preferences`, so it follows the
    account rather than the browser.
  - Everyone sees a lapsing licence; only administrators get the "Enter licence
    key" button, because only they can act on it. An installation that has never
    entered a key at all is shown to administrators only — nobody else needs a
    notice about a purchase they cannot make.
- **Seat limit.** When the licence carries a `maxUsers`, creating a **new** user
  past it is refused — in the settings form and again in `sync.php`, so the
  ceiling is not a client-side suggestion. Accounts that already exist are never
  touched: a licence downgrade stops the team growing, it does not shrink it, and
  an installation with no licence at all has no ceiling.
- **CLI:** `php ccrm license status`, `php ccrm license set <key-or-token>`,
  `php ccrm license check`.
- **`php ccrm update` now checks the licence first** and refuses to pull, with an
  explanation of what to do about it, when the licence is missing, expired,
  revoked, suspended, unverifiable, or has not been confirmed with the licence
  server for over 30 days. It fails **open** when licensing is not configured in
  the build and when CCRM is not installed on that machine yet — refusing an
  update is also a way to leave a customer stuck on a version with a security
  bug.

### 🔐 The trust model

The licence server (a Craft CMS channel plus a small module — see
[`docs/licensing/README.md`](docs/licensing/README.md)) never simply answers
"yes". It returns a **signed token**: the claim, base64url-encoded, plus a
signature over exactly those bytes. CCRM ships the matching public key and
verifies offline. Three consequences worth stating plainly:

- **A substituted licence server is useless.** Hijacked DNS, a TLS-terminating
  proxy, or a replacement server cannot mint a licence — none of them hold the
  private key.
- **Editing the CCRM database is useless.** The stored token is re-verified from
  scratch on *every* read, so setting `licenses.expires_at` to 2099 by hand
  changes a display column and nothing else. Every decision reads the claim
  inside the signature; nothing reads the columns beside it.
- **A vendor outage is invisible to the customer.** Because the cached token
  carries its own proof, the last verified claim stays authoritative while the
  licence server is unreachable — for 30 days as far as *updates* are concerned,
  and for ever as far as the application is concerned, which is never affected.

Three replay defences, because a signed token is a bearer object: the claim names
the installation it was issued for, an online check sends a nonce the server must
echo back inside the claim, and a token is refused if it was issued earlier than
the one already stored.

The signature covers the *encoded* claim rather than the decoded object, which
removes every canonicalisation question — there is exactly one byte string to
verify and it is the one that arrived. The algorithm is taken from the
**configured key**, never from the token, so `alg` cannot be used to choose a
weaker verifier or "none". Ed25519 and RSA-2048 are both supported, and the key
setting accepts several keys at once so one can be rotated across a release.

### 🗄️ Backend

- **`api/license_client.php`** — the shared, output-free library every consumer
  goes through (the HTTP endpoint, `sync.php`'s seat check, and the CLI update
  gate), so the rules cannot drift between them.
- **`api/license.php`** — status for any signed-in user, activation / re-check /
  removal for administrators. Answers in stable status and error **codes**, never
  sentences: all three interface languages live in `translations.ts`, and a raw
  server string can never reach the UI untranslated. Activation is rate-limited
  per IP and globally (`license_attempts`), because a key is a guessable secret.
- **The full licence key is never sent to the browser** — not even to an
  administrator. It is a bearer credential for the vendor's server, the admin
  already has their copy, and an endpoint that hands it back would turn any XSS
  into licence theft. The masked form is enough to confirm which key is
  installed.
- **`licenses`** (one row, `id` pinned to 1) and **`license_attempts`** added to
  `api/schema.php`, so every install path creates them identically.
- **An opaque installation id** is minted once into `system_settings`, letting
  the licence server bind a key to an install without CCRM ever sending anything
  about the customer's data. It is deliberately not derived from the hostname or
  database name — a licence that breaks when a customer migrates their server is
  a support ticket, not a control.
- **Background re-checks are throttled to one every 12 hours** across all
  requests, claimed atomically so N concurrent requests produce one call to the
  licence server rather than N, and a failed check is not retried for 20 minutes.

### 🧪 Testing

- **`src/utils/license.test.ts`** (in `npm run test:unit`) covers the pure
  decision layer: parsing an untrusted payload, when the banner appears, how a
  dismissal is keyed to the situation rather than to the banner, and the seat
  arithmetic. It includes the case where the QA harness's generic
  `{ success: true, data: [] }` must **not** be read as a licence state.
- **`scripts/test/license-verification.php`** — 94 checks over the licensing
  backend, run against real keypairs in both algorithms and a throwaway MySQL
  database: signature verification, tamper and replay rejection, algorithm
  confusion, key rotation, malformed tokens, how a licence-server answer is read,
  the whole state machine, and the seat ceiling. Deliberately outside `npm test`,
  whose contract is "no Docker, no PHP, no database required".
- The Craft-side signer and the CCRM-side verifier were exercised **against each
  other** with the shipped scripts, which is what caught a `getopt` bug in
  `mint-offline-token.php`: `--expires 2027-08-28` was silently discarded and the
  script cheerfully issued a token expiring 1970-01-01.

### 📄 Docs

- **[`docs/licensing/README.md`](docs/licensing/README.md)** — the trust model,
  the claim format, the Craft channel's fields, keypair generation, and the
  operating notes (revoking a licence, a customer who rebuilt their server, one

## [1.9.1-Jackfruit] — 2026-08-27

Audit and hardening pass over the 1.9 Invoices & Price Offers section. It shipped
looking unlike the rest of the app, with a backend that never reached the deployed
artifact, and several release blockers.

### 🚑 Fixed — release blockers

- **`sync.php` was a PHP parse error.** The 1.9 invoicing read-block was inserted one
  closing brace short, so the whole sync endpoint — every read and write, for every
  section — returned a fatal. The CRM could not load or save any data at all.
- **The backend never shipped.** `public/` is the deploy source of truth (`php ccrm
  update` copies `public/` → `dist/` → docroot), but the 1.9 work only touched the root
  mirrors. The built artifact contained no `invoices_offers` sync, no invoicing tables
  in `schema.php`, and none of `superfaktura.php` / `idoklad.php` /
  `generate_pdf_template.php`. Every price offer would have been silently discarded.
- **Settings crashed.** The Invoicing tab referenced `FileText`, `Building2`,
  `Sparkles` and `cn` without importing them; the Unified Entries tab lost its
  `settingsAction` / `settingsActionId` props. Both threw on render.
- **Settings → Projects lost its content.** The `<ProjectSettings />` block was deleted
  while the tab stayed in the navigation, leaving it blank. Restored.
- **`VERSION_CODENAME` was dropped** from `version.ts` while `RagAiView` still imports
  it at module scope, naming the RAG assistant `undefined`. Restored — and
  `increment-version.js` no longer overwrites the file (it also used to reset any
  codenamed version to 1.1.0).
- **`api/chat_rag.php` was a PHP parse error** in the root mirror, from an unterminated
  `try` block introduced in v1.8.14. The RAG chat endpoint returned a fatal.
- **Root and `public/` backends had diverged in both directions** — root ahead on
  invoicing, `public/` ahead on the v1.8.26–v1.8.30 RAG work. Reconciled to the union
  (the autonomous-agent warehouse RAG block was merged into the newer copy), so a
  publish no longer leaves the checkout dirty and blocks the next `ccrm update`.
- **`pushStateToServer` argument shift.** The post-re-login replay passed its options
  object into the `nextInvoicesOffers` slot, corrupting the replayed payload.

### 🐛 Fixed — correctness

- iDoklad price offers always failed: the connector accepted only
  `create_price_offer` while the UI sends `create_estimate`. Both names now work.
- iDoklad ignored its Sandbox toggle and issued **real** documents against the
  production API. It now uses the sandbox identity and API hosts.
- iDoklad created a duplicate contact for every document; it now looks the partner up
  by IČO (or name) first, and fails loudly instead of posting with `PartnerId: 0`.
- iDoklad sent DIČ in the VAT-number field; it now sends IČ DPH. The country id is
  derived from the client's country instead of being hardcoded.
- The AI custom template was never applied — `CustomAiOfferTemplate` was rendered
  without its `customTemplate` prop, so every "AI" document came out with defaults.
- Invoices were titled "Predbežná cenová ponuka": the title was fixed at mount and
  never re-derived when the document type changed.
- Document numbers came from `list.length + 1`, handing out duplicates as soon as a
  document was deleted. They are now derived from the highest number in the series.
- Editing an already-issued external document re-posted it to the accounting service.
- Deleting your only price offer silently failed: the mass-delete circuit breaker
  treated an explicit, user-initiated delete as an empty/stale push. Explicit delta
  deletions may now empty an ordinary table; delete-by-omission is still blocked, and
  `strict` tables (users, project types) are still never emptied.
- Dates were derived from `toISOString()` (UTC), filing anything issued after local
  midnight under the previous day. They now use the local-time helpers.
- Money and dates ignored `systemCurrency` and the user's language — `EUR` and `sk-SK`
  were hardcoded throughout, including on the printed document.

### 🔒 Fixed — security

- **Accounting credentials were exposed.** `INVOICING_INTEGRATIONS` (SuperFaktúra API
  key, iDoklad client secret) was stored in plaintext and sent unmasked to every
  authenticated client on every sync, whatever their role. Secrets are now encrypted at
  rest and masked outbound, matching `INTEGRATIONS_CONFIG`; saving a masked value keeps
  the stored secret, and the connectors decrypt server-side.
- Company billing settings and invoicing integrations were wiped whenever a client
  saved settings without including them. Omitted keys are now left untouched.
- **Another company's identity was baked in as the default.** An unconfigured tenant
  issued price offers carrying a real third party's name, address, IČO/DIČ/IČ DPH,
  phone numbers, email and client references. All removed — the templates omit whatever
  Settings leaves blank, and the section shows a setup notice instead.
- AI-generated template colours are validated as hex before reaching a style attribute,
  section ids are allow-listed, and text fields are length-capped.
- External API calls now have connect and total timeouts with abort handling, instead
  of hanging the wizard indefinitely.

### 🎨 Changed — UI/UX unified with the rest of the app

- The section rendered inside its own `p-4 sm:p-8 max-w-7xl mx-auto` wrapper on top of
  the layout's own padding, making it narrower than every other section and
  double-padded. It now uses the standard `space-y-6 pb-16` root.
- **It was the only screen in the app with a dark theme.** It carried 157 `dark:`
  utilities, and Tailwind v4 resolves those against `prefers-color-scheme` (the
  `darkMode: "class"` config is never loaded — there is no `@config`), so on an OS in
  dark mode this one section went dark while everything else stayed light. All removed,
  and the `dark:` variant is re-pointed at the app's own `data-theme` mechanism so a
  stray utility cannot do this again.
- The section header now matches the app's standard title row instead of a bespoke card.
- Native `<select>` elements replaced with the app's `CustomSelect`.
- `alert()` replaced with the app's toast; confirmations are localised.
- Full EN/SK/HU translation — the section was hardcoded Slovak and ignored its
  `systemLanguage` prop entirely.

### ✨ Added

- Editing an existing document, and changing its status from the list — status was
  filterable but could never actually change, so the win-rate KPI was permanently 0 %.
  Win rate is now measured over decided offers only.
- Print/PDF outputs just the document. `window.print()` used to spool the whole app
  shell — sidebar, header, modal backdrop — around it.
- New documents are pre-filled from Settings → Invoicing (VAT rate, payment terms,
  warranty, duration, start date, next steps, USP cards) instead of carrying hardcoded
  demo line items and a hardcoded price range.
- Settings → Invoicing gained the default VAT rate, project duration, start date,
  next-steps text and USP card editor that the 1.9.0 notes promised but never rendered.
- The warehouse picker is searchable instead of listing only the first 8 products.
- Line-item discount is editable, and the document shows the net/VAT split.
- iDoklad documents fetch and store the server-rendered PDF, linked from the preview.
